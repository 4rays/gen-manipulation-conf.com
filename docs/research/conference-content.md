## Purpose and audience

The conference examines algorithmic influence, exploitation of cognitive vulnerabilities, human autonomy, mental well-being, and democratic resilience. The source highlights disproportionate risks for young people and aging populations.

It connects technical engineering, cognitive neuroscience, social policy, and governance to explore socio-technical safeguards and cognitive resilience.

Stated audience: scholars, policymakers, and industry practitioners. Format: expert keynotes, thematic discussions, panels/round tables, and collaborative breakout sessions. Cross-sector networks and future interdisciplinary research are stated goals.

These are usable subject areas, not a confirmed branded track taxonomy:

- Generative AI, influence, and human agency.
- Cognition, attention, and mental well-being.
- Law, governance, and harmful manipulation.
- Interdisciplinary safeguards and public welfare.

### Aimen Taimur

Speaker card: Researcher, University of Amsterdam. Bio: Postdoctoral Researcher at the Institute for Information Law (IViR), University of Amsterdam, and the AI, Media and Democracy Lab. Research: AI, fundamental rights, cognitive freedom, manipulation, and technology governance.

**Conflict:** the agenda says “VU Amsterdam”, a different university. Do not silently normalize it.

Talk and full abstract supplied: **The Evolution of EU Regulation of Harmful Manipulation**. Covers the evolution from consumer/data protection and fundamental rights to AI governance, the EU AI Act, persuasion versus manipulation, recognizable harm, and cognitive autonomy.

### Anass Sedrati

Researcher, KTH. The source describes work across technology, regulation, and governance; vice director of the SWEWIN research center; and KTH representation in Sweden's AI standardization committee.

Programme role: welcome and conference context. Named conference contact; do not infer sole organizer or copyright owner from contact status.

Profile: https://www.kth.se/profile/anass

### Anders Hedman

Associate Professor and Docent in Media Technology and Interaction Design, KTH. Research and teaching connect philosophy, computer science, psychology, AI, philosophy of mind, and human-computer interaction.

Talk and full abstract supplied: **Manipulation Without a Manipulator: AI, Conferred Agency, and Social Reality**. Examines manipulation and intentional agency, companies' design choices, and socially conferred model agency. Its philosophical claims are the speaker's thesis, not claims the website should present as consensus.

Profile: https://www.kth.se/profile/ahedman
